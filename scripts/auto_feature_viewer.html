What you see. Left: the real frame as recorded. Right: the 3DGS render at the same camera pose under the global fit, in the same lens geometry, so a fixed point should sit at nearly the same pixel in both. Candidates are physical points that the matcher found in both images in several key frames (every fifth accepted frame), that triangulate consistently from the renders, and whose real observations agree with the global registration. They were built from SuperPoint keypoints matched by LightGlue (MINIMA cross-modal weights for real vs render), chained through the key renders, with unchained matches attached where a candidate is expected.

Two kinds of point. A round dot is a match made by the matcher in that frame, independently in the real image and the render. A diamond is a position propagated by CoTracker3 from the nearest matched frame (left panel); in the right panel the diamond sits where the candidate's 3-D point projects. Propagated points only appear with all frames switched on, which also makes the slider cover every accepted frame instead of the key frames. The number is the candidate's rank in the list and is the same in both panels.

What to check. Use the crops under the view: real on top, render below, one column per matched frame, crosshair on the matched point. Accept if every column shows the same fixed physical point (wall marks, floor tape corners, fixtures, door and window edges). Reject if it sits on a gate, mat, chair, box, cable or anything that may have moved, or if the columns disagree. In all-frames mode, watch whether the diamond stays on the same physical point as the frames advance.

Decisions are written immediately to research/fixed_features/auto_features.json. Accepted candidates, with their matched and propagated positions, join the manual features in the fits once the pipeline is rerun.
